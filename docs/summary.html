SUMMARY

Acme Case Study

What we fixed, what is left, what to check with Acme

In short

  • Acme sent 3 complaints and 1 bonus ask. All four are covered.
  • The 3 complaints are built, tested and committed. The bonus is built halfway.
  • Every AI change was measured first. Every code change had tests first.
  • Where the AI is still wrong, the form says so. No wrong extraction is silent.
  • Where we had to guess what Acme means, the guess is a setting. Acme's answer is a settings change, not a deploy.
  • No database changes. Nothing is pushed yet. The pull request is the last step.

The four asks

1

PDF extraction

Built

"The extracted data is often wrong. Fields come out incorrect or empty, and sometimes the upload errors out completely."

What was really wrong

  • Our own schema crashed on an empty VAT ID. That was 6 of 15 uploads.
  • Nobody had written down what "right" means: net or gross, shipping, alternatives.
  • The AI did the sums and nothing checked them.
  • Part of one quote is a picture, so no text reader saw it.
  • A bigger model made the same mistakes. It was not the model.

What we did

  • Built an eval first: 5 quotes, hand-checked answer keys, the real model, 3 runs each.
  • Empty fields are allowed and shown as missing. No more crashes.
  • The prompt now says what right is. The reply follows a fixed schema.
  • Totals, VAT and gross are added up and checked in code.
  • OCR reads the text in pictures on every upload.
  • The form shows alternatives apart, marks what doesn't add up, and takes the department from the profile.

Not fully covered

What it costs
  • Before the fixes: $0.0039 per quote. Now: $0.0069 per quote.
  • About $7 per 1,000 quotes. One wrong request a person has to fix costs more.
  • Most of the cost is the 2,000-group list pasted into every call. Taking it out cuts tokens about 11 times, with the same score in a test run.
  • Decided: keep the page-picture call. Bad data costs more than about a cent per upload.
  • A cost engine prices every setup from the measured runs: python -m evals.cost.report.

2

Negotiated prices

Built

"When someone creates a request, I want the system to suggest the matching articles we already have on catalog, so we actually use those agreements."

What was really wrong

  • Nothing matched a request line to a catalogue article. The feature did not exist.

What we did

  • Built a search eval first: 59 wordings, 40 that should match and 19 that must get nothing.
  • A line now shows "Did you mean" with the article and its negotiated price. One tap switches, with Undo.
  • One sure match or nothing. No list of maybes.
  • Works in German and English, with no AI call, in about 10 ms.
  • Only the signed-in company's catalogue is searched.
  • What the person did with each suggestion is logged, so Acme can count how often its prices are used.

Proof

  • 39 of 40 wordings matched. 0 wrong suggestions at the strict setting.
  • On wording it had never seen: 6 of 10, still 0 wrong.

Not fully covered

  • The eval answer key is our draft. It needs a human check.
  • The same article is sold by up to 11 suppliers. We pick the quote's own vendor, then the cheapest. Acme has to confirm.
  • Suggestions show on a new request, not yet when editing one.
  • Keeping a pricier offer is silent. The approver is not told yet.
Why words and not embeddings
  • Embeddings ranked the right article first 40 of 40 times.
  • But near misses scored as high as real matches. With no wrong suggestion allowed, only 11 would be shown.
  • Word matching with a small German to English map gave 39 of 40. So it ships, and embeddings stay in the code behind a flag.
  • The shown setting is a little looser: two "must be nothing" wordings now get a suggestion of the right kind. A suggestion is only shown, the person switches by hand.

3

Commodity groups

Built

"Cable ties always under Electrical Engineering, printer toner under IT Hardware, small software subscriptions under Office Supplies. Non-negotiable."

What was really wrong

  • Only the AI picked the group, from 2,000 groups pasted into the prompt.
  • There was nowhere to put a company rule. A hint in a prompt is a suggestion, and "non-negotiable" needs code.
  • Typed requests got no group help at all.
  • 2 of Acme's 3 group names don't exist in the list.

What we did

Line text"Kabelbinder 200mm"
→
Company rulekeywords, German and English
→
Group locked"Set automatically"
→
Save checkwrong group is corrected
  • Rules live in Acme's settings. No other company sees them.
  • A rule hit wins over the AI, on every create and every edit.
  • A wrong group sent by the form or an agent is quietly corrected and logged.
  • Only a buyer or admin can override. That is logged too.

Proof

  • Acme's 3 examples land in the right group in German and English, through the form, a PDF and the API.
  • Near misses hit nothing: a cable drum, a printer, a big ERP licence.
  • A test proves Acme's rules never reach another company.

Not fully covered

  • "IT Hardware" and "Office Supplies" don't exist. We use Hardware and Office Equipment as stand-ins until Acme confirms.
  • "Small" software is a line up to €500. That number is our placeholder.
  • "Subscription" also catches a magazine under €500. Needs Acme's real wording.
  • No screen to manage rules. They are set through the settings API.
  • Requests where no rule matches still get the old AI pick. A smaller classifier is started, not committed, with no eval yet.
Why we did not add Acme's groups to the list
  • The group list is shared by every company. A change made for one customer on a guess lands on all of them.
  • So Acme is fixed with settings only. The shared parts change once we know it's right.
  • All three open points are settings. Acme's answer is a settings change.
How a rule matches
  • Both sides are cleaned the same way: lower case, umlauts spelled out, punctuation to spaces.
  • "toner" hits "Tonerkartusche" and "Druckertoner". "cable tie" hits "cable ties".
  • Several rules on one line: list order wins. Lines hit different rules: the highest-value line decides.
  • A request holds one group, not one per line. Per-line groups need a database change, so later.

★

Bonus: approval limit

Half built

"Anything below €2,000 shouldn't need approval at all."

Committed

  • The limit is a company setting. Acme's is 2000. No limit keeps today's behaviour.
  • The rule that decides, with 43 tests: €1,999.99 is approved, €2,000.00 still needs a buyer.
  • An "Auto" label on the Requests and Approvals lists.

Written, not committed

  • Auto-approval when a request is saved, and back to pending when an edit takes it over the limit.
  • One older test still expects a buyer to approve a small request. It has to be updated before this is committed.

Safe by design

  • The limit is checked against the net total added up in code. The total the form sends is never trusted.
  • A request whose lines don't add up is never approved automatically.
  • A buyer's no stays a no.

Not covered

  • Splitting one order into small ones to stay under the limit.
  • Any member can close a request before approval. Found here, logged as its own fix.

Where the AI is still wrong, the form covers it

  • We did not aim for a perfect extraction. We aimed for no wrong answer the person can't see.
  • The eval counts it: wrong answers with no warning in the form. 0 of 3.
  • It also counts false alarms: right quotes that got a warning. 0 of 12.

How we would run it

  • Send the questions for Problem 3 first. It is the one Acme called non-negotiable.
  • Ask for 20 to 30 real quotes, failing and working, scans included. Run the eval on them before changing anything.
  • Show Acme the form with one of their own quotes. Watch what they correct.
  • Read the stored extractions after two weeks. Every correction becomes a new eval case.

Later

Not asked by Acme, or too big for the timebox. Logged so nothing is lost.

Problem 1, extraction
  • The quote next to the form, so checking a value takes one glance.
  • Click a field and see where on the page the value came from.
  • One-tap fixes for a mismatch.
  • Confidence per field, so the person checks the unsure ones first.
  • Swap a line for one of the quote's alternatives.
  • Vendor tied to whoever owns the VAT ID and bank details.
  • The group list out of the extraction prompt. About 11 times fewer tokens.
  • A human review queue, the eval in CI, weekly spot checks in production.
  • A reasoning log for every AI decision: value, where, why.
Problem 2, negotiated prices
  • Suggestions when editing a request.
  • A flag for the approver: "+€X vs catalogue" on lines that ignored a match.
  • The strict version: a check at submit, with a reason for keeping a pricier offer. Only if Acme makes catalogue use a rule.
  • A report of what Acme buys off-catalogue, from the logged "no match" lines.
  • A link from the order line to the article in the database, to measure agreement use.
Problem 3, commodity groups
  • A screen for Acme's admin to add, edit, try and remove rules.
  • For requests with no rule: search a short list of groups, then a small AI pick from that list, with its own eval.
  • Show the top 3 groups and learn from which one people pick.
  • Clean the padded list: 2,000 groups are 380 real names.
  • A group per line, not one per request.
  • An override button for buyers in the form.
Bonus, approval limit
  • Commit auto-approval at save, with the old test updated.
  • A screen to set the limit.
  • Use the gross check at save too, so right quotes stop waiting for a buyer.
  • Stop requesters closing a request before approval.
  • Catch split orders.

How we worked

  • Measure first. An eval before any AI change. Tests before any other change.
  • Real model. Evals call the live model, never a fake one. Every run is saved with the prompt, the model and the date.
  • One step, one commit. 40 commits on the branch. The why is in each message.
  • No database changes. New data goes into the settings and the activity log.
  • Settings over patches. Acme's quirks live in Acme's settings, not in the product.
  • Backend tests: 323 pass, 1 old test to update for the uncommitted auto-approval. At the start: 12.
  • Frontend tests: 62 pass. At the start: none.
  • Extraction eval: 24 saved runs. Search eval: 7 saved runs.
  • Branch case-study/improvements. Nothing pushed.

Left before hand-off

  • Update the old test and commit auto-approval at save.
  • One end-to-end check: the 5 quotes and Acme's 3 complaints, through the real app.
  • Re-run the eval signed in as Acme. The last such run was before the latest fixes.
  • Open the pull request. Its description is the hand-off.
